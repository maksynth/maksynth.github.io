THE PERSON BEHIND THE PROJECT

ABOUT ME

MAK Synth started as an independent project driven by my interest in electronics, engineering, and music. What began as an idea gradually developed into a fully functional educational synthesizer through ten different hardware prototypes.

Working independently, I took responsibility for the entire development process — from designing circuits and custom PCBs to modeling enclosures, programming the hardware, and developing an educational curriculum. I wanted to build more than an instrument. I wanted to make the engineering behind electronic music accessible to people with little to no prior experience.

BEHIND THE HARDWARE

WHAT WENT INTO BUILDING IT

Developing MAK Synth meant taking on responsibilities across multiple disciplines. I had to consider not only whether something worked, but also how it could be manufactured, assembled, taught, and used by someone else.

01 / ELECTRONICS

PCB DESIGN

Designed custom printed circuit boards to organize the synthesizer's electronics and simplify hardware integration. Developed separate boards for the buttons and potentiometers, connecting the physical controls to the Arduino.

02 / PRODUCT DESIGN

CAD & PROTOTYPING

Designed and iterated the synthesizer's physical enclosure and internal layout. Used CAD and 3D printing to experiment with component placement, ergonomics, assembly, and manufacturing constraints.

03 / EDUCATION

CURRICULUM DEVELOPMENT

Created an educational curriculum introducing beginners to sound synthesis, electronics, and signal flow. Designed the learning experience around hands-on experimentation rather than requiring previous musical or engineering knowledge.

04 / COMMUNITY

COMMUNITY COLLABORATION

Coordinated with local makerspaces and public libraries to explore opportunities for hosting educational workshops. Worked through logistical challenges involving software installation, hardware accessibility, and workshop environments.

05 / SOFTWARE

HARDWARE & SOFTWARE INTEGRATION

Developed the Arduino firmware and SuperCollider sound engine, connecting physical controls to digital sound generation. Worked across hardware, communication protocols, and software debugging to create an interactive instrument.

06 / ITERATION

TESTING & REFINEMENT

Tested the synthesizer across different computers and operating systems, documenting installation issues and troubleshooting problems. Used feedback and practical constraints to guide design decisions throughout development.

THE BIGGER PICTURE

MORE THAN A SYNTHESIZER.

MAK Synth has taught me that engineering extends far beyond making something function. It involves understanding people, balancing competing constraints, simplifying complex systems, and designing products that others can actually use.

Most importantly, building this project independently has allowed me to experience the entire product development process — from an initial idea to a physical instrument designed to teach others.
