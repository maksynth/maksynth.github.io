How does MAK SYNTH work?

A controller you build connects to a sound engine on your computer, all talking over a USB cable to get the sound you hear.

The steps behind what you see & hear

  1. 1The controller

    Twelve keys and six knobs sit on a custom board wired to an Arduino. Pressing a key pulls its pin low, and each knob is read as a number from 0 to 1023.

    • <0,5,0>
    • <1,4,812>
    • <0,5,1>
    • <0,0,0>
    • <1,2,640>
    • <0,0,1>
    • <1,5,301>
    • <0,7,0>
    • <1,3,455>
    • <0,9,0>
    • <0,9,1>
    • <1,0,977>
    • <0,2,0>
    • <1,1,214>
    • <0,2,1>
    • <1,4,690>
    • <0,11,0>
    • <0,11,1>

    2Serial messages

    The firmware sends a short message when something changes: <0,5,0> for a key press, <1,4,812> for a knob. They travel over USB at 115,200 baud, with keys debounced and knobs smoothed.

  3. 3The sound engine

    SuperCollider reads those messages on your computer. Each key starts a voice, and each knob changes one setting in real time.

  4. 4Oscillators and filter

    A voice starts as a sine, sawtooth, triangle, pulse or noise wave, then passes through a resonant low-pass filter set by the Cutoff and Resonance knobs.

  5. 5The envelope

    Attack, decay, sustain and release shape each note’s volume over time, so a note can swell in slowly or stop the moment you let go.

  6. 6Sound and visualizers

    Voices are mixed with headroom so chords don’t clip, then sent to your speakers. On screen, an oscilloscope, a spectrum analyzer and an envelope graph follow along.

The six controls & what they do

Every knob maps to one idea from synthesis that you’ll find on almost any synthesizer. Open one to turn it and see what the engine does with it.

The five waveforms included

The waveform is the raw tone a voice begins with, before the filter and envelope shape it.

  • Sine

    A pure, smooth tone with no added harmonics.

  • Sawtooth

    Bright and buzzy, rich in harmonics: a classic synth sound.

  • Triangle

    Soft and round, between a sine and a pulse, with a few quiet harmonics.

  • Pulse

    Hollow and reedy, built from a square-edged wave.

  • Noise

    Unpitched hiss, useful for percussion and texture.

The learning process

Each control changes the sound the moment you move it, and the visualizers react at the same time, so the link between what you do and what you hear is easy to follow.

Oscillators, envelopes, filters, and signal flow work the same way on almost every synthesizer, either hardware or software. Everything you’ll learn can be applied to whatever you do next!
